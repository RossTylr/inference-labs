## What must not be claimed

- That a rule's published accuracy is a property of the rule: HOLDS (cells 2, 12, 17, 18, 20, 34, 35, 38, 39, 41; rules.py lines 129 to 155, 344 to 345 and 363 to 369, shown at app lines 68 and 71). Every published sensitivity, specificity and predictive value is given with the population it was measured in: the MPTT-24 and the Military Sieve with the UK military registry, its size and its share of Priority 1 (cells 12, 17); the MPTT-24 with the civilian registry beside the military one (cells 34, 35, 39); the ABC score with its 596 patients and their transfusion rate (cell 20) and with the later cohort of 2,438 (cell 38). Cell 2's technical register states the point as algebra, and cells 34, 38 and 39 show it: the same rule in two populations gives two operating points and two predictive values. The app's published caption ends "Each figure belongs to its registry, not to the rule." Three limits:
  - Cell 38 calls the ABC score's fall from 75% to 36% sensitivity a decay, after the background file. The cell itself says E-90 is a different population with its own definition of massive transfusion, and neither source shows a change over time in one population. The source notes (wjs_ml2023) also describe E-90's cohort as an institutional registry of massive transfusion protocol activations, which the register row does not; if the cohort was selected by activation, its 0.36 answers a different question from E-31's 75%. The fall may be claimed only as a lower figure in a different population and definition.
  - A figure computed on the generator is the rule's in the generator's population. Axis 2 of the scorecard (cell 41) says the MPTT-24 "flags 16.2 points more of the casualties than are in need". That follows from the generator's mix, in which 16.7% of the 100,000 need an intervention. In the military registry, where 47.6% were Priority 1, the same sensitivity and specificity flag almost exactly as many as are in need (E-09: 0.667 × 0.476 + 0.301 × 0.524 = 0.475). The over-flagging, and the Brier score beside it, may be claimed only for the generator's mix.
  - E-01 and E-09 describe the same cohort: 3,654 adults in the UK military registry with complete data, 2006 to 2013, 47.6% Priority 1. The MPTT-24's military accuracy was therefore measured on the patients its parent rule, the MPTT, was derived from by logistic regression (E-02), and axis 4 (cell 41) says the MPTT-24's thresholds descend from that rule. Cells 12 and 34 and the app's caption give both facts in different places but do not join them. Cell 34's fall from 66.7% to 53.5% may be claimed as what happened when the rule left the population it was fitted on, not as a measure of the difference between military and civilian casualties alone.
- That the MPTT-24 has been validated on pre-hospital observations: HOLDS (cells 4, 12, 13, 19, 40, 41, 43; baseline.py lines 23 to 26 and 194 to 196; rules.py lines 154 to 155, 295 and 297 to 300, shown at app line 71). Cell 19 states that it has not, with E-11's 16.7% and 37.2%. Cell 12 states that E-09 was measured on first recorded hospital respiratory rate and registry GCS. Every diagnosis row of the scorecard names "generator physiology fitted to hospital observations" in its metric, the rows marked before hospital included, and axis 6 says the generator cannot show what moving to the point of injury would do. The app's published caption opens "measured on first recorded hospital observations and registry GCS, not at the point of injury (E-09, E-11)". Running the flowchart on a synthetic casualty in the app demonstrates the rule; it claims nothing about its accuracy there. Axis 5's "a medic can follow it at the point of care" is a stated readability, not a validation. One limit: cell 19's "has not been validated on pre-hospital observations" rests on E-11, which describes the one validation the register holds. It may be claimed as: the validation in the register was not on pre-hospital observations, and the register holds no other.
