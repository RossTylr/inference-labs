## What must not be claimed

- That a rule's published accuracy is a property of the rule: HOLDS (cells 2, 12, 17, 18, 20, 34, 35, 38, 39, 41; rules.py lines 129 to 155, 340 to 341 and 357 to 363, shown at app lines 68 and 71). Every published sensitivity, specificity and predictive value is given with the population it was measured in: the MPTT-24 and the Military Sieve with the UK military registry, its size and its share of Priority 1 (cells 12, 17); the MPTT-24 with the civilian registry beside the military one (cells 34, 35, 39); the ABC score with its 596 patients and their transfusion rate (cell 20) and with the later cohort of 2,438 (cell 38). Cell 2's technical register states the point as algebra, and cells 34, 38 and 39 show it: the same rule in two populations gives two operating points and two predictive values. The app's published caption ends "Each figure belongs to its registry, not to the rule." Two limits:
  - Cell 38 calls the ABC score's fall from 75% to 36% sensitivity a decay, after the background file. The cell itself says E-90 is a different population with its own definition of massive transfusion, and neither source shows a change over time in one population. The fall may be claimed only as a lower figure in a different population and definition.
  - A figure computed on the generator is the rule's in the generator's population. Axis 2 of the scorecard (cell 41) says the MPTT-24 "flags 16.2 points more of the casualties than are in need". That follows from the generator's mix, in which 16.7% of the 100,000 need an intervention. In the military registry, where 47.6% were Priority 1, the same sensitivity and specificity flag almost exactly as many as are in need (E-09: 0.667 × 0.476 + 0.301 × 0.524 = 0.475). The over-flagging, and the Brier score beside it, may be claimed only for the generator's mix.
- That the MPTT-24 has been validated on pre-hospital observations: HOLDS (cells 4, 12, 13, 19, 40, 41, 43; baseline.py lines 23 to 26 and 194 to 196; rules.py lines 154 to 155 and 293 to 296, shown at app line 71). Cell 19 states that it has not, with E-11's 16.7% and 37.2%. Cell 12 states that E-09 was measured on first recorded hospital respiratory rate and registry GCS. Every diagnosis row of the scorecard names "generator physiology fitted to hospital observations" in its metric, the rows marked before hospital included, and axis 6 says the generator cannot show what moving to the point of injury would do. The app's published caption opens "measured on first recorded hospital observations and registry GCS, not at the point of injury (E-09, E-11)". Running the flowchart on a synthetic casualty in the app demonstrates the rule; it claims nothing about its accuracy there. Axis 5's "a medic can follow it at the point of care" is a stated readability, not a validation.

## Epistemic checks

- Accuracy quoted for the wrong cohort or setting: HOLDS (cells 12, 13, 17, 18, 20, 24, 34, 37, 38, 45; rules.py lines 142 to 155; app line 71). Each published operating point is given in its own cohort and with its observations: the MPTT-24 and the Military Sieve in the military registry and the MPTT-24 in the civilian one, all on first hospital observations (E-09 to E-11); the ABC score in its original cohort and in E-90's. The calibration checks (cells 13 and 37) hold the text reading to E-09, because the text's 'responds to voice' stands for E-09's GCS below 14 (E-08); the figure reading is shown beside it and not held to it, as R-2.3 requires. The beliefs quoted in cell 24, about 65% and 90%, are given with their setting, with the one-in-five starting share that notebook 03 marks as an assumption, and with the civilian studies they rest on. E-88's figures in cell 45 belong to a civilian field triage protocol in ten Dutch hospitals and are not set against the MPTT-24.
- Proxy data read as the real thing: HOLDS (cells 1, 3, 4, 12, 13, 17, 20 to 23, 29, 30, 32, 33, 36, 37, 40, 41, 43, 44; app lines 73 to 78). Every result on the generator is called synthetic or a mechanism. Cells 4, 12, 17 and 36 say that the generator was built to reproduce E-09, so the coded rule meets E-09 by construction and the agreement tests the generator, not the rule. Cell 17 says the 3,446 casualties moved by the voice step is a number the generator sets. Its description was checked against the generator: half of those not alert respond to voice (`avpu_abnormal_shares` in params.yaml), and each Priority 1 casualty has exactly one abnormal sign (generator.py lines 246 to 263). Cell 22 shows how far the ABC results turn on the penetrating assumption: 12.8% activated against 24.3% if explosions counted. The needle share in cells 32 and 33 is marked as resting on an assumed normal shape, checked against E-104's 99%. Three limits:
  - Cell 20 says the generator's "pressure and pulse were drawn to make the MPTT-24, not the ABC score, reproduce published figures". The pulse was; the pressure was not. The MPTT-24 reads no pressure, and the generator draws systolic pressure from assumed parameters with no published target (`sbp_bleeding` and `sbp_not_bleeding` in params.yaml; generator.py lines 265 to 269). The ABC score's pressure item therefore rests on a further assumption that the cell does not list. The cell's conclusion, that the results are not a measure of the score in military casualties, stands, and more firmly.
  - E-104's figures come from CT of 100 male military cases. The background file records them as autopsy imaging, and the background pack's source notes (`docs/background/_notes.md`, harcke2007) record the 5.36 cm as the horizontal thickness and give a smaller perpendicular one that the register does not hold. The notebook says the cases were male and that the angle of insertion is ignored, but not that the scans were post-mortem or the thickness horizontal. Cell 33's 62 of 100, and its tag's "most of them", may be claimed only for the register's measurement under the assumed shape; whether most chest walls would defeat the needle depends on which thickness lies along its path.
  - Cell 34 says the field record "points the same way as the measurements; it is not a test of them", and then opens "The needle rule failed by meeting a body thicker than it was written for." The second sentence asserts the link the first declines. E-106 gives no cause of failure. It may be claimed only that the anatomy and the field record point the same way.
- Civilian data read as military: HOLDS (cells 20, 24, 28, 30, 34, 38, 39, 45; rules.py lines 16 to 17, 335 to 343 and 349 to 356, shown at app lines 68 and 70). The ABC score's 596 patients are said not to be described as military in the register, and applying the score to a military casualty is called an assumption, in cell 20 and in the app. CRASH-2 is called civilian wherever its figures meet the three-hour rule: cell 28, the figure at cell 30, the library's docstring and comment, and the app's tranexamic acid line. The scan studies behind cell 24's beliefs are called civilian. TARN is named as the civilian registry in cells 34 and 35, the figure at cell 39 and the app. E-88's Dutch hospitals and E-90's cohort are nowhere described as military. The sources described as military are military: JTTR (E-01, E-08 to E-11), E-86, E-104 and E-106.
- A diagnosis result read as a treatment result: HOLDS (cells 20 to 30, 40 to 44; rules.py lines 198 to 213 and 245 to 253). Cell 20 keeps the treatment case apart from the diagnosis case. The treatment column of the scorecard measures how often the ABC score's activation coincides with significant bleeding, stated as the stand-in for the need for transfusion. That is the accuracy of a treatment trigger against an indication; no cell reads it as the effect of transfusing. The doctrine's four items are given as predictors of massive transfusion (E-86). The one treatment result in the notebook, CRASH-2's by time to treatment (E-22), is presented as a trial result and marked exploratory and civilian. Two limits:
  - The baseline column of the treatment rows (cells 21, 42, 43) is the MPTT-24's Priority 1, a triage output, scored against significant bleeding; cell 42 says so. It may be claimed only as how often Priority 1 coincides with significant bleeding on the generator, not as a transfusion decision by the MPTT-24, which makes none.
  - Cell 29's 99.0% inside three hours counts synthetic arrival times, an assumption chosen so the generator reproduces E-22's bands (cell 28; `time_to_phec_min` in params.yaml). It says nothing about how many military casualties reach care within three hours.
