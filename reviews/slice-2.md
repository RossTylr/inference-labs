## What must not be claimed

- That a rule's published accuracy is a property of the rule: HOLDS (cells 2, 12, 17, 18, 20, 34, 35, 38, 39, 41; rules.py lines 129 to 155, 293 to 295, 345 to 347 and 363 to 369, shown at app lines 68 and 71). Every published sensitivity, specificity and predictive value is given with the population it was measured in: the MPTT-24 and the Military Sieve with the UK military registry, its size and its share of Priority 1 (cells 12, 17); the MPTT-24 with the civilian registry beside the military one (cells 34, 35, 39); the ABC score with its 596 patients and their transfusion rate (cell 20), with the later cohort of 2,438 (cell 38), and, in axis 4 and the panel's transfusion line, as accuracy that "comes from 596 patients (E-31)". Cell 2 states the point as algebra, and cells 34, 38 and 39 show it: the same rule in two populations gives two operating points and two predictive values. The app's published caption ends "Each figure belongs to its registry, not to the rule." Four limits:
  - Cell 2 says "the same $f$ therefore has a different sensitivity wherever $p(x \mid D)$ differs". The algebra above it shows that it can; a different $p(x \mid D)$ can leave the sum unchanged. The sentence may be read as "can have". Its second half, on the predictive value, is exact.
  - Axis 4 (cell 41; rules.py lines 294 to 295) and the transfusion line (lines 345 to 346) call E-31's 75% and 86% "its published accuracy" and "the score's published accuracy". The score has a second published operating point, E-90's 0.36 and 0.80, which cell 38 gives and the page does not. Both name the figures and the 596 patients, so neither presents them as the score's in general; they may be read only as the figures the score was first published with.
  - A figure computed on the generator is the rule's in the generator's population. Axis 2 (cell 41) says the MPTT-24 "flags 16.2 points more of the casualties than are in need". That follows from the generator's mix, in which 16.7% of the 100,000 need an intervention. In the military registry, where 47.6% were Priority 1, the same sensitivity and specificity flag almost exactly as many as are in need (E-09: 0.667 × 0.476 + 0.301 × 0.524 = 0.475). The over-flagging, and the Brier score beside it, may be claimed only for the generator's mix.
  - E-01 and E-09 describe the same cohort: 3,654 adults in the UK military registry with complete data, 2006 to 2013, 47.6% Priority 1. The MPTT-24's military accuracy was therefore measured on the patients its parent rule, the MPTT, was derived from by logistic regression (E-02), and axis 4 says, now citing E-08 as well, that the MPTT-24's thresholds descend from that rule. Cells 12 and 34 and the app's caption give both facts in different places but do not join them. Cell 34's fall from 66.7% to 53.5% may be claimed as what happened when the rule left the population its parent was fitted on, not as a measure of the difference between military and civilian casualties alone.
- That the MPTT-24 has been validated on pre-hospital observations: HOLDS (cells 4, 12, 13, 19, 40, 41, 43; baseline.py lines 21 to 24 and 192 to 194; rules.py lines 154 to 155, 296 and 298 to 301, shown at app line 71). Cell 19 now says the published figures "were measured on first recorded hospital values, not pre-hospital ones", with E-11's 16.7% and 37.2%, which is what E-11 records; the third review's limit on the wider "has not been validated" is resolved. Cell 12 states that E-09 was measured on first recorded hospital respiratory rate and registry GCS.
