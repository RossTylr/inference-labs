## Findings in brief

Two breaches. First, the notebook names E-85 as from civilian centres and applies its hazard to the generator's battle casualties, but the only assumption it names is the clock; it nowhere says that applying E-85 to a battle casualty is an assumption, as standing lesson 1 requires. The panel says it. Second, the last sentence of cell 34 says the fixed-rate chain "is wrong on those it was not fitted to", which the notebook's own outputs contradict at 1 and 6 hours and on the share of deaths within 6 hours. Nothing else is found to be a breach. Ten limits are recorded.

## Limits

- E-127 (cell 15; the figure in cell 16). The register row gives prehospital time as what E-127 varied and does not say where its deaths were counted. "E-127 measures before it" and "measured before hospital" are true of the time it measured, as the background file puts it, and may not be read as deaths counted before hospital. In cell 16 its shading sits on the generator's hazard, which rises through the first 30 minutes to a peak at 0.86 hours.
- E-85's hourly rates are shown as an "hourly death rate among those alive" (cell 12's published column; the stars of cell 33), which is how `params.yaml` reads them: "deaths per casualty-hour at risk", "taken as the hazard". The register row says "hourly death rate". The generator also takes E-85's haemorrhaging patients to be its casualties bleeding significantly.
- Cell 32's "used here and nowhere else" is true of running the generator with the constant-hazard option. Cells 12 and 13, in the diagnosis case as R-6.5 asks, compute the same chain from the generator's derived rate, and cell 11 calls it "the generator's constant-hazard option, written as a chain".
- `spread_words` (stochastic.py lines 101 to 109) says "a tie: no detectable difference" whenever the range across seeds covers zero. Lesson 12 defines a tie that way, but a mean across 20 seeds can detect what the range does not: the fixed-rate chain's rate at 6 hours is 0.092 points above the time-varying hazard's on average, over a range of -0.036 to +0.236 (cell 33). The phrase may be read only as "the range across the seeds covers zero".
- Axis 7's "treat all" and "treat none" (stochastic.py lines 396 to 416), and cell 39's "treating none", are net benefit's names for flagging every casualty and none for death within the hour; they are not a treatment.
- D-23's statement (axis 1 of the baseline column; cell 36) describes the generator's injured, battle and non-battle together, of whom 26.5% need a life-saving intervention. The casualties the rule is scored on here are battle casualties bleeding significantly, every one of whom needs one (generator.py line 305), and the target is death from bleeding within the hour, not need.
- The panel's treatment line gives the share of the reduction left "on the lens's line" without saying that the straight line, and so the share, is an assumption. Cell 21 and axis 4 say so.
- Cell 11 gives E-25's 884 of 1,408 for "the trials pooled by E-24" and, from E-26, that time-to-death data existed only for the obstetric trial. Both are as the register gives them. The register does not say how the two fit together, so E-25's timing may not be read as resting on trauma patients in any given share.
- The scorecard rows named "mean chance minus observed share" (stochastic.py lines 747 to 748 and 774 to 775; rows 3 and 7 of cell 38) carry as the rule's baseline its share flagged less the share who died, 0.574, under a name that speaks of a chance.
- App page 1 draws its casualties from all battle casualties, including 10 of the 327 bleeding significantly in each setting whom the generator has dead from bleeding before pre-hospital care. The panel's chances are stated as conditional on being alive then, and its truth line says only whether the casualty is bleeding.
