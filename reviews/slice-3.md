## The generator

- The generator as the notebook, library and app describe it: HOLDS (cells 1, 2, 4, 12 first paragraph, 19, 28; axis 6; frequentist.py lines 6 to 10, 17 to 18, 43, 196 to 202, 286 and 302 to 303; app line 76). Each sentence was checked against `generator.py` and `params.yaml`. 100,000 casualties, seed 42, the military cohort and every stream are `generator_sample`'s defaults (baseline.py lines 167 to 169), and the disease stream is in the default mix (43.4% of them), so "disease included" is right. The score is fitted on a different 100,000 drawn with seed 7 from the military cohort and its threshold set on them (frequentist.py lines 43 and 196 to 202). The signs are drawn from the latent state with assumed parameters (generator.py lines 29 and 307 to 343). The setting changes only the scan: the times to care are drawn without it, and it chooses the scan's accuracy and when the scan is done (lines 346 to 359). The injured are the battle and non-battle streams (baseline.py lines 77 and 172 to 174). The respiratory rate is drawn two-sided, raised by the conditions and slowed in a share of severe head injuries (lines 43 to 44 and 328 to 331). The resampling draws 357 of the injured without replacement (frequentist.py lines 301 to 318).
- The civilian cohort as cell 12 describes it: BREACH (cell 12, second paragraph). The paragraph reads: "The score was fitted on the military cohort. Measured against the military injured, on the civilian cohort, whose heart and respiratory rates are assumed to move less with bleeding, tension pneumothorax and sepsis, the score's sensitivity falls from 71.8% to 53.2% and the rule's from 62.7% to 52.5%, while both gain specificity." Every figure is right (cell 13, rows 8, 14, 6 and 12), and specificity does rise, from 65.8% to 70.9% for the score and from 73.7% to 77.1% for the rule. The clause is the only account the notebook gives of how the civilian cohort differs, and it stands in the sentence as the reason for both changes. On the generator it cannot be the reason for the second. The response scale of 0.35 multiplies only the effects of bleeding, tension pneumothorax, sepsis and septic shock (generator.py lines 315 to 331; `cohort_physiology_civilian`), and in the civilian cohort every casualty with one of them needs an intervention: bleeding and tension pneumothorax are themselves needs, and sepsis arises only in the disease stream, which the civilian cohort does not have (lines 298 to 305; `cohort_mix_civilian`), so the clause's sepsis never occurs in it. The scale therefore acts only on casualties in need and cannot move specificity. The civilian cohort also has a heart rate 6 beats a minute lower in every casualty (line 327; `heart_rate_shift: -6`), which no cell states. Re-drawing the civilian cohort with one change at a time separates the two. With the military physiology the score has sensitivity 70.8% and specificity 66.0% and the rule 63.2% and 73.7%, within a point of the military injured, so the mix moves little. The smaller response alone moves the score to 58.9% and 66.0% and the rule to 56.3% and 73.7%: most of the fall in sensitivity and none of the gain in specificity. The lower heart rate alone moves them to 65.7% and 70.9% and to 60.2% and 77.1%: the rest of the fall and all of the gain. A reader would conclude that blunted signs explain why both methods flag fewer of those not in need; on the generator a lower resting heart rate does. What may be claimed is the paragraph's figures; that the civilian cohort differs from the military injured in its mix, a lower heart rate and a smaller response to bleeding and tension pneumothorax, all assumptions; and that the smaller response accounts for most of the fall in sensitivity and none of the gain in specificity. The paragraph is new since the first review, so the finding is not MISSED.
