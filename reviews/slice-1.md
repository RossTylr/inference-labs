## Epistemic checks

- Accuracy quoted for the wrong cohort or setting: HOLDS (cells 2 to 9, 12 to 16, 32 to 34, 41 to 43; bayesian.py lines 18 to 21, 79 to 91 and 35 to 38; app lines 73 to 78). Each scan figure is used in its own setting: E-16 before hospital, E-14 in hospital. The one cross-setting use is the failure case, where it is the point and is named as such. The baseline rows quote no published MPTT-24 figure, and cell 41 says the rule's accuracy was measured on first hospital observations (E-09, E-11), so the rows marked before hospital do not describe the rule at the point of injury. Cell 32 calls the borrowed accuracy "the commonest version" of the failure: a judgement, not a measured frequency. Four limits:
  - Cell 5 no longer says the pre-hospital service was physician-staffed. The partyka2022 brief says it was, and that E-16 cannot support military pre-hospital use for that reason among others. The hook (cell 0) has a medic scan the casualty and cell 16 answers it with E-16's figures. Cell 5's "using these figures for a military casualty is an assumption" covers this, but the 25% and 96% may be claimed only as accuracy measured with physicians scanning, carried to a medic by assumption.
  - E-15 records free-fluid specificity of 95% (92.6 to 96.6) in hypotensive patients against 98% (97.5 to 98.1) in normotensive adults. The lens updates on a systolic pressure of 90 or less and then on the scan with the pooled 98%, and the generator draws the scan without regard to pressure (generator.py lines 352 to 358). Cell 35's "combining them is sound here" is scoped to the generator, which is correct; in hospital, a belief after a positive scan in a casualty at or below 90 mm Hg holds only on the generator.
  - Cell 41's "its published accuracy" is E-09's figure in the UK military registry, 66.7% and 69.9%, to which `params.yaml` holds the military cohort. The cell does not name that population. On the generator's injured the default reading reaches 62.7% and 73.7%, and the text reading 64.1% and 73.1% (baseline.py lines 183 to 199).
  - Cell 43's comparison with the baseline holds partly by construction. The generator draws the scan with the accuracy the lens assumes (generator.py lines 352 to 358), and the target, abdominal bleeding, is what the scan looks for; the rule reads no scan and was built for another target. That the lens "does better on both counts" in hospital may be claimed only as a mechanism on the generator, which is how the cell puts it.
- Proxy data read as the real thing: HOLDS (cells 16 to 20, 27, 28, 33 to 37, 41 to 43; bayesian.py lines 24, 32 to 33 and 167 to 170; app lines 26 to 27 and 73 to 78).   - Scorecard row 8 is 0.998 because the CRASH-2 prior on its own gives 0.997; the fixture's interval runs from 0.41 to 1.54 (cell 28), so the row measures the prior, not the fixture, and says nothing about tranexamic acid.
  - The pre-hospital belief in cell 34 meets the generator's count because the generator draws its scans with E-16's accuracy (generator.py lines 352 to 358, `params.yaml` scan_accuracy_pre_hospital), so that agreement holds by construction.
  - Cell 16 says that on the generator "nothing has to be assumed about the starting share: it can be counted". Within the generator that is so. The share may be claimed as counted, not as known.
  - The baseline's Brier score and calibration row (0.422 and 0.314) depend on how many of the generator's battle casualties are bleeding into the abdomen (12.8%) and how many the rule puts in Priority 1 (44.2%). They describe the generator's mix, not the rule.
