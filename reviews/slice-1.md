Each scan figure is used in its own setting: E-16 before hospital, E-14 in hospital. The one cross-setting use is the failure case, where it is the point and is named as such. The statements that the 25% describes a scan called positive and that the paper does not make clear how indeterminate scans enter its table are the register's own (partyka2022 brief). E-31 supplies only the 90 mm Hg threshold; its accuracy is not quoted. Cell 32 calls the borrowed accuracy "the commonest version" of the failure; that is a judgement, not a measured frequency, and may be claimed only as one. The new baseline rows quote no published MPTT-24 figure. Cell 41 says they are computed on a generator whose physiology is fitted to the rule's published accuracy on first hospital observations, so the rows marked before hospital do not describe the rule at the point of injury.
- Proxy data read as the real thing: HOLDS (cells 16 to 20, 27, 28, 33 to 37, 41 to 43; app lines 26 to 27 and 73 to 78). Every result on the generator is called a mechanism, and the CRASH-3 fixture is called synthetic, random and clinically meaningless where it is used. The app's closing caption now exempts the trial's absolute reduction and the rules' published accuracy, each given with its id. Cell 41 calls the baseline column a mechanism that says nothing about how the rule performs on real casualties. Four limits on what may be claimed:
  - Scorecard row 8 is 0.998 because the CRASH-2 prior on its own gives 0.997; the fixture's interval runs from 0.41 to 1.54, so the row measures the prior, not the fixture, and says nothing about tranexamic acid.
  - The pre-hospital belief in cell 34 meets the generator's count because the generator draws its scans with E-16's accuracy (generator.py lines 278 to 283), so that agreement holds by construction.
  - Cell 43's comparison with the baseline holds partly by construction too. The generator draws the scan with the accuracy the lens assumes, and the target, abdominal bleeding, is what the scan looks for; the rule reads no scan and was built for another target. That the lens "does better on both counts" in hospital may be claimed only as a mechanism on the generator, which is how the cell puts it.
  - The baseline's Brier score and calibration row (0.425 and 0.321) depend on how many of the generator's battle casualties are bleeding into the abdomen and how many the rule puts in Priority 1. They describe the generator's mix, not the rule.
- Civilian data read as military: HOLDS (cells 5, 21, 25, 29; bayesian.py lines 25 to 27, 90 to 91 and 483 to 492, shown at app line 34; app lines 76 to 77). The scan studies are called civilian. Cell 5 adds that no military figure for the scan was found and that using the studies for a military casualty is an assumption; the register holds no military scan figure (E-14 to E-17). The relative risk of bleeding death by delay is named as CRASH-2's wherever it is applied to the casualty: cell 29, the library's docstring and comment, and the app's treatment caption. Each place states that using a civilian trial's figures for a military casualty is an assumption. - A diagnosis result read as a treatment result: HOLDS (cells 29 to 31, 41 to 43; bayesian.py lines 273 to 277, 395 to 397 and 476 to 506, shown at app lines 32 to 34 and 58). The app labels the product "Belief times relative reduction (mechanism)". The caption directly beneath the metrics describes it as the belief multiplied by CRASH-2's relative reduction for the delay. - Tranexamic acid by delay: HOLDS (cells 29, 30; bayesian.py lines 90 to 99 and 483 to 492; app lines 32 to 34). All three bands match E-22, and the log-normal shape is stated as an assumption. Within an hour, 7.7% of the placebo group and 5.3% of the tranexamic acid group died from bleeding, and 2.4 points is the difference, as E-22 records. Three limits on what may be claimed:

The caption's "relative reduction ... that CRASH-2 found" must then be read as a signed quantity, and the page shows that late treatment was associated with more deaths only through the negative product.
