# 04 — Decision trees

**The lesson in one line:** a tree is a rule the data wrote; one tree is readable and fragile, many trees are strong and opaque, and the choice between them is the choice the whole field keeps making.

## Thinking

**The single tree came last.** In E-90 ten methods were tried on the same patients and the plain decision tree was one of two that failed the bar. In E-91 the forest of trees came first. That is the lesson of the lens in two rows: a tree's weakness is its variance, and averaging many trees removes it at the cost of the one thing a tree had, which is that you could read it.

**PECARN is what a good tree looks like.** Six questions, forty thousand children, a separate validation set, and a result that held (E-89). It is a tree that became a rule and is used at the bedside. What made it work was not the algorithm but the size of the cohort, the prospective design, and the choice to optimise for not missing an injury. The notebook should grow a tree on a few hundred rows, watch it change with each resample, and then show what forty thousand rows buys.

**Three variables are nearly enough.** E-30 gets 0.84 from age, pressure and conscious level, and 0.85 from twice as many. Most of the information is in the first few splits. That is why fast-and-frugal trees (E-59) work at all, and why the MPTT-24 can be four questions long.

**A predictor that is not available yet.** The leading predictor in E-92 is the Injury Severity Score. That score is calculated after the injuries have been found and coded, usually after imaging or surgery. A model that uses it to predict transfusion "early" is using information from later. This is an inference from how the score is constructed, not a claim made in the paper, and it should be checked against the full text before the notebook asserts it. It is nonetheless the right failure to teach: a tree will split on whatever helps, including the answer.

**Quality, not accuracy, is the gap.** E-91's pooled figures are high and its quality scores are low: no study met every reporting item and most models were never tested elsewhere. High accuracy on the data that built the model is the default, not an achievement.

## What must not be claimed

- That PECARN was derived by recursive partitioning, without checking the paper. It is widely described so, but the abstract does not say it.
- That a forest's 0.89 will hold on military casualties. None of these models was built on them.
