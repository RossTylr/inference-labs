## proppr2015 [EuropePMC abstract] Holcomb JB, Tilley BC, Baraniuk S, Fox EE, Wade CE, Podbielski JM et al. JAMA 2015;313(5):471-482. doi:10.1001/jama.2015.12
Pragmatic phase 3 RCT, 12 North American level I centres, Aug 2012-Dec 2013, 680 pts predicted to need massive transfusion: 1:1:1 (n=338) vs 1:1:2 (n=342) plasma:platelets:red cells.
24h mortality 12.7% vs 17.0%, diff -4.2% (95% CI -9.6 to 1.1), P=.12. 30d 22.4% vs 26.1%, diff -3.7% (-10.2 to 2.7), P=.26. Neither significant.
Secondary: exsanguination death in 24h 9.2% vs 14.6%, diff -5.4% (-10.4 to -0.5), P=.03. Haemostasis 86% vs 78%, P=.006. No difference in 23 prespecified complications.

## pamper2018 [EuropePMC abstract] Sperry JL, Guyette FX, Brown JB, Yazer MH, Triulzi DJ, Early-Young BJ et al. NEJM 2018;379(4):315-326. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1802345
Cluster-randomised phase 3, air medical transport, thawed plasma vs standard care. 501 pts: 230 plasma, 271 standard.
30d mortality 23.2% vs 33.0%, diff -9.8 points (95% CI -18.6 to -1.0), P=.03. Consistent across 9 prespecified subgroups. KM curves separate from 3h after randomisation. No safety differences. Funded by US Army MRMC.

## matters2012 [EuropePMC abstract] Morrison JJ, Dubose JJ, Rasmussen TE, Midwinter MJ. Arch Surg 2012;147(2):113-119. doi:10.1001/archsurg.2011.287
Retrospective observational, Role 3 surgical hospital southern Afghanistan (UK and US registries), 896 consecutive combat injury admissions who got >=1 unit red cells; 293 got TXA (603 not - derived).
Unadjusted mortality 17.4% TXA vs 23.9% no TXA (P=.03) despite TXA group more severely injured (mean ISS 25.2 vs 22.5, P<.001).
Massive transfusion (>=10 units) subgroup: 14.4% vs 28.1% (P=.004); TXA independently associated with survival OR 7.228 (95% CI 3.016-17.322) and less coagulopathy (P=.003).
Observational: association only.

## snyder2009 [qxmd abstract page] Snyder CW, Weinberg JA, McGwin G, Melton SM, George RL, Reiff DA, Cross JM, Hubbard-Brown J, Rue LW, Kerby JD. The relationship of blood product ratio to mortality: survival benefit or survival bias? J Trauma Feb 2009. https://read.qxmd.com/read/19204508/

## zech2018 [READ full text, PLOS Med] Zech JR, Badgeley MA, Liu M, Costa AB, Titano JJ, Oermann EK. Variable generalization performance of a deep learning model to detect pneumonia in chest radiographs: a cross-sectional study. PLoS Med 2018;15(11):e1002683. doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1002683
158,323 chest radiographs, 3 sites: NIH (112,120), Mount Sinai (42,396), Indiana (3,807). Pneumonia prevalence 34.2% MSH, 1.2% NIH, 1.0% IU. Ranking by hospital alone gives AUC 0.861 on joint MSH-NIH test set. Joint-trained model: internal AUC 0.931 (0.927-0.936), external (IU) 0.815 (0.745-0.885), P=.001. Internal better than external in 3 of 5 comparisons. CNN identified the hospital system for 99.95% (NIH) and 99.98% (MSH) of radiographs; department within MSH 100%. Models can exploit site cues tied to prevalence, so internal test overstates real-world performance.

## jts_dcr2019 [READ, JTS CPG PDF] Joint Trauma System. Damage Control Resuscitation, CPG ID 18, 12 July 2019 (rapid updates June and August 2023). https://jts.health.mil/assets/docs/cpgs/Damage_Control_Resuscitation_12_Jul_2019_ID18.pdf
Blood first; whole blood preferred (low-titre group O); else components about 1:1:1; avoid crystalloid. TXA: 2 g IV/IO only if started within 3 hours of injury (later dosing linked to higher mortality). Permissive hypotension: SBP about 100 (90-110) before surgical control; >110 with isolated brain injury. Calcium 1 g with first unit, then after about every four units.
Massive transfusion = >=10 units red cells or whole blood in 24h. Predictors: 3 of 4 of SBP<100, HR>100, haematocrit<32%, pH<7.25 predicts about 70% risk; all 4 about 85%. Other indicators: above-knee amputation, multiple amputations, penetrating chest/abdominal injury, positive FAST in >2 regions, lactate>2.5, INR>=1.5, base deficit>6.
Cites PROPPR (fewer 3-hour bleeding deaths, no 24h/30d difference), CRASH-2, early blood (about 36 min from injury), MATTERs (about 14% vs 28% in massive transfusion), and a later military analysis showing more venous thromboembolism with TXA.
